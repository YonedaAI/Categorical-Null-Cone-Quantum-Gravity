
### Referee Report

**Manuscript Title:** Ordered probes at a marked null cut (Supplement to *Categorical Null-Cone Quantum Gravity*)

---

### 1. Overview and Scope

The submitted supplementary note analyzes an operational two-probe protocol at a marked null corner (cross-section/cut) of a causal diamond. Specifically, it constructs an effective two-dimensional chart $(Q, P)$ where $Q = \log(K/K_*)$ is the logarithmic area charge and $P = -K\gamma$ is its canonical conjugate generating area dilations. On this chart, the note develops a unitary Weyl-type algebra of ordered operations—a phase shift conditioned on log-area and a coherent dilation of area—and evaluates their ordered expectation values in a Gaussian state. It then investigates the geometric transport under causal diamond inclusion (both in flat spacetime and in a vacuum pp-wave background) and proves a no-go result: exact unitary dilation of area cannot be realized within any non-trivial bounded area sector (such as the finite-dimensional sectors argued by Chandrasekaran).

Crucially, the manuscript explicitly characterizes its scope as a *conditional chart construction and kinematic probe model*, rather than claiming full, unconstrained gravitational quantization. It carefully delineates the necessary boundary decoration, spectator assumptions, and spectral requirements.

### 2. Technical Evaluation of Mathematical and Physical Correctness

#### A. Sign of the Canonical Potential and Symplectic Structure
* In Section 1, the boost contribution to the symplectic potential is taken as $\theta = K\,d\gamma$ on the uniform slice. 
* Writing $Q = \log(K/K_*)$ and $P = -K\gamma$, the differential identity is:
  \[
  K\,d\gamma = d(K\gamma) - \gamma\,dK = d(K\gamma) + (-K\gamma)\frac{dK}{K} = d(K\gamma) + P\,dQ.
  \]
  Modulating by the exact differential $d(K\gamma)$, the potential is canonically $P\,dQ$.
* Setting the fundamental Poisson bracket $\{Q, P\} = 1$, we obtain:
  \[
  \{K, P\} = \{K_* e^Q, P\} = K_* e^Q \{Q, P\} = K,
  \]
  and since $\gamma = -P/K$,
  \[
  \{\gamma, K\} = \left\{-\frac{P}{K}, K\right\} = -\frac{1}{K}\{P, K\} = \frac{1}{K}\{K, P\} = 1.
  \]
* The sign conventions for the canonical coordinates, symplectic potential, and Poisson brackets are fully consistent and exact.

#### B. Area Boost vs. Dilation
* The manuscript correctly distinguishes between:
  1. The flow generated by $K$: $\dot{\gamma} = \{\gamma, K\} = 1$, $\dot{K} = 0$, which acts as a relative rapidity shift leaving the area invariant;
  2. The flow generated by $P$: $\dot{K} = \{K, P\} = K$ (scaling $K \mapsto e^a K$) and $\dot{\gamma} = \{\gamma, P\} = -\gamma$ (scaling $\gamma \mapsto e^{-a}\gamma$).
* The note properly highlights that while Chandrasekaran's construction uses the area $K$ to generate relative boosts, the probe protocol requires the dilation conjugate $P$, which is a distinct physical and algebraic operation.

#### C. Operator Algebra and Gaussian State Phases
* In Section 2, the unitary operators $L_b$ and $S_a$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R}_+, dk)$ satisfy $S_a L_b = e^{iab} L_b S_a$, intertwining via $JF(q) = (K_* e^q)^{1/2} F(K_* e^q)$ to the standard position and translation operators on $L^2(\mathbb{R}, dq)$.
* For the ordered word $W(a,b) = M(b)U(a)$ and the normalized Gaussian state $\psi(q)$ defined in Eq.~(6):
  * The density $\overline{\psi(q)}\psi(q+a)$ has real Gaussian center at $q_0 - a/2$, width $\sigma$, prefactor $\exp[-a^2/(8\sigma^2)]$, and relative phase $\exp[i p_0 a/\hbar]$.
  * Performing the Fourier Gaussian integral with $e^{ibq}$ yields:
    \[
    \langle W(a,b)\rangle_\psi = \exp\left[-\frac{a^2}{8\sigma^2} - \frac{\sigma^2 b^2}{2}\right] \exp\left[i b(q_0 - a/2) + \frac{i p_0 a}{\hbar}\right].
    \]
  * In the numerical check ($q_0 = p_0 = 0$, $\sigma = 1$, $a = b = \sqrt{\pi}$), $\langle M(b)U(a)\rangle = -i e^{-5\pi/8}$, whereas the opposite order $\langle U(a)M(b)\rangle = e^{i\pi} \langle M(b)U(a)\rangle = +i e^{-5\pi/8}$. Both the analytical integration and numerical values are correct.

#### D. Tensor Spectator Assumption
* In Section 4, the inclusion $(D_R, C_R) \subset (D_S, C_R)$ models the retained corner as an uncoupled tensor factor: $\mathcal{H} \otimes \mathcal{B}$ with marked observables $A \otimes 1$.
* The author explicitly notes the limitations of this model: if boundary constraints at the cut link the marked degree of freedom with the enlarged diamond degrees of freedom, or if the cut frame is discarded upon gluing, the tensor spectator assumption fails. Treating this as an explicit kinematic assumption rather than a theorem of full general relativity is mathematically sound and conceptually transparent.

#### E. Plane-Wave Geometry, Central-Ray Waist, and Optical Expansion
* The plane-wave metric $ds^2 = -2\,du\,dv + dx^2 + dy^2 + \kappa^2(x^2 - y^2)\,du^2$ has $R_{uu} = 0$, confirming it is an exact vacuum solution.
* Along the central worldline $x = y = 0$, $u = v = t$, the diamond tips are $p = (0,0,0,0)$ and $q_U = (U,U,0,0)$.
* The outgoing null cone from $p$ has central generator $v = x = y = 0$ parametrized by $u$. The past lightcone from $q_U$ at $x = y = 0$ contains the null ray $u = U$, where $v$ runs from $U$ down to $0$. Their intersection occurs exactly at $(u = U, v = 0, x = 0, y = 0)$. Thus, for diamond $D_R$, the waist along the central ray is precisely located at affine parameter $u = R$ (and $u = S$ for $D_S$).
* The Jacobi matrix $B(u) = \kappa^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\sinh(\kappa u), \sin(\kappa u))$ yields ray separation area density $j(u) = \det B(u) = \kappa^{-2}\sinh(\kappa u)\sin(\kappa u)$.
* Using the identity $\sinh(z)\sin(z) = \sum_{m=0}^\infty (-1)^m \frac{2^{2m+1} z^{4m+2}}{(4m+2)!} = z^2 - \frac{z^6}{90} + \frac{z^{10}}{113400} - \dots$, the expansion contains only powers $z^2, z^6, z^{10}, \dots$ (with odd powers of $z^2$, meaning powers $z^4$ and $z^8$ vanish identically).
* Therefore:
  \[
  \frac{j(S)}{j(R)} = \frac{S^2}{R^2}\left[1 - \frac{\kappa^4(S^4 - R^4)}{90} + O((\kappa S)^8)\right].
  \]
  The location of the waist, the cancellation of the leading quadrupole tide at order $\kappa^2$, and the expansion through order $O((\kappa S)^8)$ are exact.

#### F. Bounded Cutoff and Cutoff Projection
* In Section 5, if $K_{\mathrm{sec}} \ne 0$ is a bounded operator and $\mathcal{U}(a) K_{\mathrm{sec}} \mathcal{U}(a)^* = e^a K_{\mathrm{sec}}$, norm preservation of unitary conjugation requires $\|K_{\mathrm{sec}}\| = e^a \|K_{\mathrm{sec}}\|$, which is impossible for $a \ne 0$.
* Similarly, projecting onto $q \le q_{\max}$ turns the translation $U(a)$ ($a > 0$) into a proper isometric shift onto a strictly smaller subspace ($q \le q_{\max} - a$), destroying co-isometry and preventing the compressed operators from forming a unitary group.
* This rigorously illustrates the obstruction to implementing continuous exact dilations in the finite-dimensional or discrete spectra implied by Chandrasekaran's macroscopic diamond bounds.

### 3. Novelty, Prior Art, and Assumptions

* **Prior Art & Citations:** The manuscript accurately cites Chandrasekaran (2026), Chandrasekaran–Prabhu (2019), Klauder (2010), and Harte–Drivas (2012). The mathematical derivations correctly build upon and complement these references.
* **Clarity of Assumptions:** The manuscript is exceptionally candid regarding what is proven versus what is assumed. It does not claim to have solved the non-perturbative constraint equations of quantum gravity; rather, it isolates the precise corner chart conditions, boundary decorations, spectator structure, and spectral properties necessary for ordered area probes to be physically meaningful.
