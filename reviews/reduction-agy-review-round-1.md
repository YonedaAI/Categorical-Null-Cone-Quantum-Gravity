### 1. General Overview and Summary of Results

The manuscript investigates the algebraic mechanics of boundary frame reductions, comparing classical reduction, derived (Koszul) intersections, quantum normalizer quotients, and infinitesimal gauge cochains. The guiding physical motivation is the fate of boundary charges and local observable algebras at null cuts or diamond corners in gravitational theories (as analyzed by Chandrasekaran, Donnelly–Freidel, and Long). Rather than asserting formal claims directly on infinite-dimensional, curved gravitational phase spaces, the paper establishes a series of exact theorems within a finite-dimensional polynomial mechanics framework with linear translation constraints.

The central results include:
1. **Classical and Quantum Reduction for Paired Frames (§3, §5):** Explicit computation of the reduced Poisson algebra and the associative/star normalizer quotient $\mathcal{N}(L)/L$ of the polynomial Weyl algebra for two boundary frames under a diagonal translation constraint. It demonstrates that the relative coordinate and its conjugate momentum survive as an unconstrained canonical pair.
2. **Derived Fiber vs. Observable Algebra (§4, §6, §7):** Demonstration that redundant constraint equations induce negative-degree Koszul cohomology classes ($H^{-1}$) without modifying the degree-zero classical or quantum observable algebras. Furthermore, in the combined Koszul–Chevalley–Eilenberg complex, ineffective Lie algebra generators contribute positive-degree classes ($H^{+1}$), whose product with Koszul excess classes creates nilpotent degree-zero classes.
3. **Graph Cycle Obstructions (§8):** Extension to an arbitrary finite oriented graph of boundary matching constraints, establishing that the quantum quotient is non-trivial if and only if the signed constraint level around every cycle vanishes. Inconsistent cycle levels collapse the entire quantum left ideal to $\mathcal{A}$, annihilating the quotient.
4. **Descent and Level Transport (§9, §10):** Formulation of necessary and sufficient conditions for algebra morphisms to descend to normalizer quotients, showing that real $\hbar$-shifts obstruct descent across fixed-level constraints unless intertwined by unitary boundary phase operators on generalized states.

The paper is exceptionally well-conceived, mathematically rigorous, and lucidly written.

### 2. Detailed Technical Assessment

#### 2.1. Pair-of-Frames and Quantum Weyl Normalizer Quotient (§3, §5)
* **Classical Reduction (Proposition 1):** The choice of adapted canonical coordinates $r = q_L - q_R$, $k = p_L$, $s = q_R$, $n = p_L + p_R$ decouples the presymplectic form into $dr \wedge dk + ds \wedge dn$. The Hamiltonian vector field $X_n = \{ \cdot, n\} = \partial_s$ generates free translations along the gauge orbit. The Poisson normalizer quotient $\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cl}}(I)/I$ correctly recovers $\mathbb{C}[r, k]$ with $\{r, k\} = 1$.
* **Quantum Normalizer Quotient (Theorem 1):** The definition of the normalizer of the left ideal $L = \mathcal{A}N$ as $\mathcal{N}(L) = \{a \in \mathcal{A} : La \subset L\}$ is algebraically standard and physically sound. The proof that $\mathcal{N}(L)/L \cong B$ (where $B$ is the Weyl algebra generated by $R_0, K$) via normal ordering in $S$ and $N$ is exact. 
* **Adjoint Structure:** The manuscript astutely addresses the failure of $*$-stability for the kinematical left ideal $L$ (since $(SN)^* = NS = SN - i\hbar 1 \notin L$). Presenting the quotient via the centralizer $\mathcal{Z}(N)$ modulo $\mathcal{Z}(N) \cap L$ ensures a strictly well-defined involution on physical observables.

#### 2.2. Koszul and Infinitesimal Cochain Calculations (§4, §6, §7)
* **Derived Zero Fiber (Proposition 2 & Theorem 2):** The analysis of the Koszul complex $K(R; f)$ for redundant constraints $(n, n)$ cleanly isolates the excess intersection class $\varepsilon = e_2 - e_1 \in H^{-1}$. This provides a cautionary lesson for derived geometry in gauge theory: derived fibers retain syzygies between constraint presentations that have no counterpart as physical local degrees of freedom.
* **Combined Koszul–Chevalley–Eilenberg Complex (Proposition 3):** The formulation of the differential in equation (3) on $\mathbb{C}[r,k,s,n] \otimes \Lambda(e, v) \otimes \Lambda(c, w)$ correctly decouples into contractible pairs $(s_j \xrightarrow{\partial_{s_j}} c_j)$ and $(e_j \xrightarrow{n_j - \lambda_j} n_j - \lambda_j)$, leaving the cohomology $\mathbb{C}[r, k] \otimes \Lambda^\bullet F_\mathbb{C} \otimes \Lambda^\bullet F_\mathbb{C}^*$. The observation that a degree $-1$ Koszul class $v$ and a degree $+1$ ghost class $w$ pair to yield a non-trivial nilpotent degree $0$ class ($vw \ne 0$, $(vw)^2 = 0$) is an acute insight into why naive BRST/cochain cohomology can retain artifacts of redundant gauge presentations.

#### 2.3. Graph Obstruction and Cycle Consistency (§8)
* **Corollary 1:** The identification of the incidence operator $T: \mathbb{R}^{E_G} \to \mathbb{R}^{V_G}$ and the characterization of consistent levels via the cycle condition $\sum_{e \in C} \lambda_e = 0$ is completely sound. The dimension of the unconstrained quantum quotient is precisely $c$ (the number of connected components of $G$).
* **Quantum Collapse:** The triangle counterexample ($1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 1$ with $\lambda_{31} = \hbar \alpha$) illustrates the stark difference between classical tree consistency and global quantum consistency: local consistency on every proper subgraph fails to prevent global quantum collapse ($L_\lambda = \mathcal{A}_\hbar(V)$). The manuscript rightfully notes that interpreting such an obstruction as a gravitational anomaly requires proving the absence of allowed local counterterms/recalibrations.

#### 2.4. Adjoint and Level Transport (§9, §10)
* **Transport Criteria:** Equations (4) and (5) establish the proper categorical criteria for algebra morphisms between constrained systems: $\alpha(L_1) \subset L_2$ and $\alpha(\mathcal{N}(L_1)) \subset \mathcal{N}(L_2)$. The sufficient criterion that $\mathcal{A}_2 \alpha(L_1) = L_2$ is concisely verified.
* **Noncompact Boundary and Distributional States (§10):** The discussion of physical states on $L^2(\mathbb{R}^2)$ accurately points out that $N = -i\hbar \partial_s$ has purely continuous spectrum at $0$. The averaging functional $\eta\psi(r) = \int_\mathbb{R} \psi(r, s) ds$ is shown not to extend to a bounded operator on the kinematical Hilbert space via the scaling family $h_b(s) = b^{-1/2}h(s/b)$. The intertwiner $U_t = e^{its}$ cleanly formalizes the transport between shifted levels $\lambda$ and $\lambda + t\hbar$.

#### 2.5. Physical Scope, Context, and Exposition (§1, §2, §11, §12)
* The framing of the paper is well-calibrated. It avoids overclaiming that a toy polynomial translation model solves the corner problem in general relativity; rather, it uses the solvable model to establish necessary consistency checks that any prospective categorical or algebraic quantization (e.g., Chandrasekaran's null-cone quantization or Long's relational diamond reconstruction) must satisfy.
* The distinction between first-class constraints generating gauge transformations and second-class constraints requiring Dirac brackets (§11) provides essential physical context preventing misapplication of the model.

### 3. Specific Corrections and Improvements

The manuscript is in very strong technical shape. A few minor points, clarifications, and typographical adjustments should be addressed:

1. **Section 6, Theorem 2, Item 2 (Cohomology Grading):**
   * *Location:* Page 4, Theorem 2, Item (2).
   * *Remark:* The text states: *"...tensored with $\Lambda^\bullet F_\mathbb{C}$, placing $F_\mathbb{C}$ in degree $-1$."* 
   * *Correction/Clarification:* To prevent any potential ambiguity with the total degree of the exterior algebra, explicitly state that $\Lambda^p F_\mathbb{C}$ sits in cohomological degree $-p$ (so the total complex is supported in degrees $-(m-a) \le j \le 0$). In Corollary 1, the text emphasizes degree $-1$ ($H^{-1}$ has rank $|E_G| - |V_G| + c$), which represents the generators/relations; mentioning that the higher exterior powers occupy degrees down to $-(|E_G| - |V_G| + c)$ would ensure complete clarity.

2. **Section 7, Equation (3) and Proposition 3 (BRST / Lie Cochain Sign Conventions):**
   * *Location:* Page 5, Section 7, before Proposition 3.
   * *Remark:* When introducing the differential $D$ in equation (3), the text notes that $c_j, w_i$ anticommute with the odd Koszul generators $e_j, v_i$ under the total-degree sign rule.
   * *Recommendation:* Explicitly write out the graded commutator formula for the differential on a product, e.g., $D(a b) = (Da)b + (-1)^{|a|} a (Db)$, where $|a|$ is the total degree (Koszul degree plus Lie cochain degree). While standard to experts, specifying this rule eliminates any confusion regarding signs when applying $D$ to mixed terms like $s_j^k$ or $v_i w_j$.

3. **Section 8, Paragraph 3 (Triangle Example Generators):**
   * *Location:* Page 6, Section 8, second paragraph below Corollary 1.
   * *Text:* *"The sum $Q = q_1 + q_2 + q_3$ is invariant under all edge shifts. Its conjugate can be taken to be $K = (p_1 + p_2 + p_3)/3$."*
   * *Remark:* Note that while $K$ as written is conjugate to $Q$ with $\{Q, K\} = 1$, on the constrained surface where $p_2 = p_1 + \lambda_{12}$ and $p_3 = p_1 + \lambda_{12} + \lambda_{23}$, $K = p_1 + \frac{1}{3}(2\lambda_{12} + \lambda_{23})$. A brief parenthetical remark clarifying that $K$ acts as the unconstrained momentum offset by a constant central character will assist readers tracking the affine origin shifts discussed in §6.

4. **Section 9, Transport of Constraint Ideals:**
   * *Location:* Page 7, Section 9, second paragraph.
   * *Text:* *"If $a\in\cN(L_1)$ and $b=\sum_i c_i\alpha(\ell_i)\in L_2$, then $b\,\alpha(a)=\sum_i c_i\alpha(\ell_i a)\in L_2$."*
   * *Remark:* This step relies on the fact that $\alpha$ is an algebra homomorphism (so $\alpha(\ell_i a) = \alpha(\ell_i)\alpha(a)$) and $\ell_i a \in L_1$ because $a \in \mathcal{N}(L_1)$. Adding one phrase—*"since $\alpha$ is a homomorphism and $L_1$ is right-normalized by $\mathcal{N}(L_1)$"*—makes the verification immediately transparent to readers unfamiliar with non-commutative ideal normalizers.

5. **Section 11, Discussion of Second-Class Constraints:**
   * *Location:* Page 8, Section 11, second paragraph.
   * *Text:* *"Treating $s,n$ as commuting generators of gauge translations on the common zero locus would instead require a translation tangent to that locus."*
   * *Remark:* It would be helpful to cite Dirac's classic formulation or Henneaux–Teitelboim alongside Chandrasekaran's Appendix D.1 to anchor the terminology for readers coming from mathematical deformation quantization rather than canonical gauge theory.

### 4. Overall Scholarly Assessment

The paper is an exemplary contribution to mathematical physics. It tackles a foundational and frequently obfuscated issue—the precise operational and algebraic meaning of boundary reduction, constraint redundancy, and quantum descent at null cuts—with crisp mathematical tools. The calculations are exact, the proofs are complete, the distinctions between classical, derived, and operator-algebraic reductions are meticulously maintained, and the limitations of the model are stated with complete intellectual honesty.

The required revisions listed above are minor pedagogical and expository refinements that can be addressed directly.
